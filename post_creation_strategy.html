A strategic UX brief covering the architecture, content type positioning, subscription model, merch commerce, AI features, and design direction for the INRCLIQ post creation experience. Based on competitor analysis, the platform's open creator shift, and the Instagram Reels engagement model.

01

The strategic shift

The original INRCLIQ model divided users into fans and creators at onboarding. Posting was a paid privilege — only upgraded creators could publish. The new direction opens posting to everyone, like Instagram, while preserving subscription monetisation and merch commerce as the primary revenue layers.

This is the right call for growth. But it means the entire post creation architecture needs to be rebuilt. The current Figma screens were designed for the old model and cannot be adapted. This brief defines what replaces them.

  • Only paid creators can post

02

The Instagram Reels lesson

Instagram's pivot is the most relevant strategic precedent. Instagram had photos and long-form video. Engagement was declining. TikTok was capturing the audience. Their response was to reposition every video format toward Reels — short, discoverable, algorithm-amplified content. Engagement reversed. Growth returned.

The principle is not about format preference. Short-form vertical video is the most powerful organic discovery mechanism on any social platform right now. INRCLIQ should apply this logic not just to Reels but to every content format — each needs a clearly defined business function, not just a media type.

Strategic principle

Instagram's shift worked because they gave each format a job. Reels drive discovery. Posts deliver value. Stories build daily intimacy. INRCLIQ needs the same architecture — six formats, six distinct jobs, one coherent creator growth system.

03

Six content types — six business functions

Excluding Story and Live which are out of scope for MVP, INRCLIQ has six content types to position: Reel, Post, Video, Audio, Poll, and Merch. Each should be designed and communicated as a distinct business instrument — not just a format option in a dropdown.

Reel
The Growth Engine
Always public. Always discoverable. Algorithm-amplified to non-followers. A creator who posts a Reel is playing for new audience — not existing fans. The Reel itself generates zero direct revenue but it is the top of the entire monetisation funnel. Without it the platform cannot grow organically. Every subscription begins with a discovery moment — and Reels are that moment.
Audience: everyoneRevenue: indirectFunction: acquisition
Post (Image / Text / Carousel)
The Value Delivery Engine
This is what subscribers pay to access. A creator's posts are the product. Free posts build the relationship and trust. Tier 1 and Tier 2 posts are the subscription value. PPV posts are the premium offering. The post format is where the 5-3-1 content strategy plays out — and where the platform's subscription revenue is generated post by post.
Audience: tieredRevenue: directFunction: retention
Video (Long-form)
The Loyalty and Upsell Engine
Long-form video builds deep loyalty with existing fans. A 20-minute behind-the-scenes or a full tutorial is not for new audiences — it is for people who already care enough to invest time. A creator who posts long-form video as Tier 2 exclusive gives fans a compelling reason to upgrade from Tier 1. This is the Patreon model applied to video — and the upsell mechanism that increases average subscription revenue per creator.
Audience: subscribersRevenue: directFunction: upsell
Audio
The Differentiation Engine
No competitor in the research scores well for audio as a first-class content format. Music creators, podcasters, spoken word artists, and DJs are completely underserved everywhere. If INRCLIQ builds audio as a proper post type — with waveform visualisation, timestamp comments, and subscription gating — it occupies a space no other platform owns. This drives a creator cohort that no competitor is actively pursuing.
Audience: tieredRevenue: directFunction: differentiation
Poll
The Engagement Amplifier
Polls are the highest-engagement-per-impression format on every platform that supports them. They require minimal creative effort but generate strong algorithm signals and direct fan input. "Vote on my next song." "Which city should I tour next?" A poll post boosts the algorithmic performance of the creator's other content — including their subscription-gated posts.
Audience: all followersRevenue: indirectFunction: amplification
Merch — Product Commerce
The Direct Revenue Engine
Creators on INRCLIQ should be able to sell physical and digital products directly from their profile and from within any post — similar to how YouTube Shopping integrates with creator content. A creator taps a product link icon in the composer, selects from their product shelf, and the post displays a shoppable card. Fans tap to view, add to cart, and checkout without leaving INRCLIQ. This removes reliance on Linktree, Shopify landing pages, and external links — keeping the entire creator-fan-purchase journey on-platform.
Audience: all usersRevenue: direct transaction feeFunction: commerce

Strategic insight

The highest-performing platforms share one principle: a lightweight default composer that expands progressively. The creator sees a blank canvas first. 06

Merch and product commerce

INRCLIQ should allow creators to sell physical and digital products directly from the platform — linked from posts, from their profile, and from a dedicated product shelf. This is the YouTube Shopping model applied to a creator-fan platform.

How it works in the composer

  • Creator taps the product icon in the composer toolbar.
  • Their product shelf opens as a bottom sheet or sidebar panel — showing product images, names, and prices.
  • They select one or more products to link to the post.
  • A shoppable card appears below the post content on publish — product image, name, and price with an Add to cart button.
  • Fans tap the card to view the product detail page, add to cart, and checkout — all within INRCLIQ.

Product shelf on the creator profile

  • A dedicated Shop tab on the creator profile — visible to all visitors alongside All, Posts, Videos, and Audio.
  • Creator can showcase physical products (merch), digital downloads (presets, samples, PDFs), and limited editions.
  • Fans can browse and purchase without navigating away from the profile.

Cart and checkout

  • Shopping cart persists across the session — fan can add products from multiple creators.
  • Checkout supports card, Apple Pay, Google Pay.
  • Order confirmation and shipping updates delivered in-app and by email.
  • Creator receives payout via their connected payment account minus the platform transaction fee.
Why this matters

Creators currently leave INRCLIQ to sell — they paste Shopify links, Linktree URLs, or Bandcamp pages in their bio. Every time a fan clicks away to buy, INRCLIQ loses the transaction, the data, and the retention signal. A native commerce layer keeps the entire creator-fan-purchase journey on-platform. That is the revenue model that sustains creator retention long term.

08

Profile tabs — aligned to business function

The current proposed tab structure — All, Photos & Reels, Long & Short Videos, Audio — has a categorisation problem. Reels appear in "Photos & Reels" but also belong behaviourally with "Long & Short Videos." This creates content overlap. Grouping by media type misses the opportunity to align the profile with the platform's business narrative.

← Current proposal — issues
  • Photos & Reels — forced pairing. Photos are static posts. Reels are short-form discovery video. Different consumption behaviours, different functions.
  • Long & Short Videos — if Reels are also in the previous tab this creates content overlap. The label reads as a technical description, not user-facing.
  • Audio — correct direction. First-class tab is a strong differentiator. Keep this.
  • No Shop tab — creators have no dedicated commerce surface on their profile.
→ Proposed — function-first tabs
  • All — everything mixed. Default landing tab.
  • Posts — images, text posts, carousels, polls. The core subscription catalogue.
  • Videos — long-form video posts and Reels together. Both are video consumption. Clean single label, no overlap.
  • Audio — music, podcast, spoken word. First-class tab. The platform's ownable differentiator.
  • Shop — creator's product shelf. Physical and digital products. Shoppable from the profile.
Wireframe skeleton — creator profile with five-tab structure
Why Audio and Shop tabs matter

The Audio tab is the platform's most strategically important UI decision at the profile level. Giving audio a first-class tab signals to music creators, podcasters, and spoken word artists that INRCLIQ was designed with them in mind. No other major platform makes this statement. The Shop tab turns the creator profile into a storefront — removing the need for external links and keeping the fan on-platform through the entire purchase journey.

The 5-3-1 content strategy — built into the platform

The 5-3-1 rule is a proven engagement framework. For every 9 posts: 5 deliver pure value, 3 build community, 1 is a direct call to action. Creators who maintain this ratio consistently outperform those who post without strategy. INRCLIQ should build it in — not as a rule, but as a visible growth tool embedded in the composer and insights dashboard.

Value content
Educational, entertaining, inspiring. Serves the audience with no ask in return. Builds trust and reach.
5
Community content
Behind-the-scenes, creator story, soft preview. Builds intimacy and connection.
3
Conversion
Direct CTA. Subscribe, buy, book. Earns the right to ask.

01   Content type tagging
Before publishing, creator optionally tags post intent — Value, Community, or Promotional. One tap, three chips. Platform tracks the mix over time and surfaces it in creator insights.
02   Content mix insight
Creator dashboard shows a 5-3-1 visualisation across recent posts. If over-indexed on promotional: "Your engagement is down — try a value post next." Soft, data-driven nudge.
03   Scheduling with ratio view
When scheduling multiple posts, the calendar highlights content mix across the upcoming queue. Promotional posts flagged amber, value posts green. Creator sees the ratio before anything publishes.
04   AI content type suggestion
AI reads the draft and suggests which 5-3-1 type it matches. "This reads as promotional. Your last 4 posts were also promotional — a value post next would protect engagement."
11

AI features in the composer

Six AI features are proposed. The competitor research highlights AI disclosure support as a recommendation across TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn and X. AI assistance in content creation is moving from a differentiator to a baseline expectation.

✦ Caption AI
Creator uploads an image. AI suggests a caption based on visual content analysis. One tap to use, fully editable before publishing. Reduces blank-page friction for visual creators.
✦ Writing improvement
Creator writes text, taps AI assist. Options: Make shorter, More engaging, Fix grammar, Translate. Pattern from LinkedIn's inline AI assist — proven and familiar interaction.
✦ Hashtag suggestions
After writing, AI analyses content and suggests relevant hashtags based on post category and trending tags on INRCLIQ. Creator selects from suggestions with one tap.
✦ Content check
Before publishing, AI scans against INRCLIQ content guidelines. Particularly important given age restriction requirements — flags content needing a warning or parental restriction.
✦ AI disclosure label
If AI was used to generate or significantly edit content, a disclosure option is available. TikTok and YouTube both support this. Increasingly a regulatory expectation. Toggle in publishing settings.
✦ Content type suggestion
AI reads the post and suggests the 5-3-1 type it matches. "This reads as promotional. Your last 4 posts were also promotional — a value post next would protect engagement."

Web-first, responsive — both breakpoints matter equally

INRCLIQ is web-first at this stage with responsive mobile support. The composer needs to be designed for both surfaces with equal care. Different interaction patterns, same feature set.

↔
Desktop — two column
Composer left two-thirds. Publishing panel right (audience, scheduling, monetisation, merch, content warning, collection). Drag and drop for media. Keyboard shortcuts: Cmd+Return publish, Cmd+S draft, Cmd+/ AI assist.
↕
Mobile — single column
Full-width composer. Publishing settings via sticky bottom toolbar. Tap to expand each section. Format selection via bottom sheet. Product link via merch shelf bottom sheet. Same features, adapted for touch.
⊞
Tablet — hybrid
At 768px–1024px, composer fills width with publishing settings in a collapsible panel. A distinct third layout state — not simply desktop or mobile scaled.
⌨
Keyboard shortcuts — web
Cmd+Return publish. Cmd+S draft. Cmd+Shift+P preview. Cmd+/ AI assist. Table stakes for a web-first publishing tool — must be defined in the design spec before development handoff.

The opportunity

Most creator platforms build a neutral publishing tool and leave creators to figure out their strategy independently. The highest-performing platforms — Patreon, YouTube, increasingly Instagram — are moving toward guided publishing that actively helps creators succeed.

INRCLIQ has the opportunity to build a composer that is as fast as Threads to open, as powerful as Patreon to monetise, and as smart as LinkedIn to refine. Positioning each content format as a business instrument — Reels for growth, Posts for retention, Video for upsell, Audio for differentiation, Polls for amplification, Merch for direct commerce — gives the platform a coherent creator growth architecture that competitors do not have.

Audio as a first-class format and Merch as a native commerce layer are the two clearest differentiators available. No competitor owns audio at the creator profile level. And giving creators a native product shelf — linked directly from posts — removes the friction that causes creators to leave for platforms with better commerce tools.
